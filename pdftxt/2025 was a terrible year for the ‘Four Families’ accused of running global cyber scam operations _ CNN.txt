Wedged between the Salween River to the west and China’s Yunnan province to the
east is the autonomous region of Kokang, where most of the local people are ethnic
Kokang – Mandarin-speaking Han Chinese.
Since the country’s independence from Britain in 1948, Chinese nationalists,
Burmese communists, Kokang warlords, ethnic rebel militias and military juntas
have struggled for inﬂuence and control over the territory, with drug production
and, more recently, gambling and telecoms scams, deﬁning Kokang’s economy.
It’s in this heady mix of lawlessness that a handful of powerful clans – known as the
“four families” of Kokang – built their empires, transforming the sleepy market
town of Laukkaing into a glittering casino city at the heart of the global scam
industry.

The global scam industry, much of it centered in Southeast Asia, is estimated to be

Torture, beatings and selling oﬀ workers were commonplace, Chinese state media
